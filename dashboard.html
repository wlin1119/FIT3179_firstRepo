Publication Timeline

How has the number of recorded board-game releases changed over time?

Data source: Sujay Kapadnis (BoardGameGeek), October 2023

OBSERVATION

Patterns over time

Board-game publishing grew slowly for decades, with fewer than 100 recorded releases per year throughout the 1980s. The pace began to accelerate in the 1990s, reaching 201 releases in 1999. By 2015, that number had surged to 733 games, the highest point in the dataset — more than three times the 1999 total.

WHY IT MATTERS

More Games, More Ways to Play

This rise marks a major expansion in the variety of board games available to players. As more titles entered the market, players gained more choices beyond traditional favourites. But did this growth simply produce more games, or did it also change how games are played?

How Many Players Can Join the Game?

Are Board Games Becoming More Complex?

Explore how the number of mechanics per game has changed across publication eras.

Data source: Sujay Kapadnis (BoardGameGeek), October 2023

OBSERVATION

From Simple Rules to Layered Gameplay

Before 1980, around 80% of games used just 1–2 mechanics. By 2010–2019, this share had fallen to roughly 45%, while games with 3–4 mechanics became much more common. Games combining five or more mechanics also gained a larger share over time.

WHY IT MATTERS

More mechanics, more possibilities

This shift suggests that modern board games increasingly combine different gameplay systems, creating opportunities for richer interactions and strategic choices. But having more mechanics does not necessarily mean a better game. The next question is how these mechanics work together.

Mechanic DNA: Which Mechanics Have Changed?

Trace the prevalence of board-game mechanics across five publication eras.

OBSERVATION

Old Favourites Fade, New Strategies Emerge

Board-game mechanics have followed different paths over time. Hex-and-Counter declined sharply from around 38% of games before 1980 to just 4% in 2010–2019. Meanwhile, Hand Management and Worker Placement became increasingly common, reflecting a shift in the types of gameplay systems used.

WHY IT MATTERS

Game Design Never Stands Still

These changing preferences suggest that board-game design continues to evolve. While traditional mechanics remain part of the hobby, newer combinations and strategic systems have gained prominence. Modern games are not simply adding more mechanics; the mechanics they use are changing too.

Do More Mechanics Make a Better Game?

Board games have evolved in the number and variety of mechanics they use. But does adding more mechanics actually lead to higher player ratings?

Do More Mechanics Mean Better Ratings?

Board games now combine a wider variety of mechanics, but does having more mechanics make a game better? This chart compares player ratings across four groups: 1–2, 3–4, 5–6, and 7+ mechanics.

OBSERVATION

Player ratings show a gradual upward trend as games incorporate more mechanics. Games with just 1–2 mechanics have a median rating of around 6.3, while those combining 7 or more reach approximately 7.2.

However, the rating distributions overlap, showing that simpler games can still be highly rated. This suggests that while games with more mechanics tend to receive better ratings, more mechanics do not automatically guarantee a better game.

Data source: Sujay Kapadnis (BoardGameGeek), October 2023

Where Are Board-Game Cafés Found?

Explore the global distribution of recorded board-game cafés by country.

Data source: Andy Matthews (Meeple Mountain), 2019

OBSERVATION

A Global Hobby, Unevenly Distributed

Board-game cafés appear across multiple continents, but recorded counts vary greatly. The United States leads with 249 cafés, followed by Canada (118) and the United Kingdom (109). Europe also shows a broad spread of cafés, with France recording 57.

WHY IT MATTERS

More Than Just a Place to Play

Board-game cafés provide spaces where people can discover games and connect with other players. Their presence across continents illustrates the international reach of tabletop gaming, while the uneven distribution raises questions about access to these spaces and differences in data coverage.

Beyond gaming cafés, crowdfunding reveals where new board-game projects attract financial support.

Where Is the Money Going?

Explore the total Kickstarter funding raised by board-game projects across countries.

Data source: Mickaël Mouillé (Kaggle — Kickstarter Projects), 2016

OBSERVATION

One Country Dominates Crowdfunding

Kickstarter funding for board games is heavily concentrated in the United States, with approximately $254.3 million recorded. This far exceeds the United Kingdom's $25.6 million and Canada's $4.7 million. Although funding spans several countries, the gap between leading markets is substantial.

WHY IT MATTERS

A Global Market, Unequal Funding

Crowdfunding gives board-game creators opportunities to reach supporters worldwide. However, the concentration of funding suggests that projects based in certain countries attract much larger financial totals. This raises another question: do these countries also launch more campaigns, or do their projects simply raise more money?

Does More Funding Mean More Campaigns?

Beyond funding totals, campaign counts reveal where board-game crowdfunding is most active.

A Global Platform, Dominated by a Few Countries

The United States leads with 8,499 campaigns, followed by the United Kingdom with 1,083. Canada and Australia follow with 408 and 172 campaigns respectively.

WHY IT MATTERS

More Campaigns Do Not Always Mean More Success

Comparing this map with the previous funding map reveals two sides of crowdfunding: how many projects are launched and how much money they raise. The United States leads both measures, suggesting a particularly large presence in this Kickstarter dataset. However, a high campaign count does not necessarily mean each project raises more money. Understanding crowdfunding success requires looking beyond the number of campaigns to funding per project and other outcomes.

Data source: Mickaël Mouillé (Kaggle — Kickstarter Projects), 2016

THE FINAL CHAPTER

The Game Is Still Evolving.

♟ ⚄ ▱
01 / GROWTH

More Than Just a Publishing Boom

Recorded board-game releases grew dramatically, reaching 733 games in 2015 in this dataset. But the expansion was not simply about producing more titles. Games also diversified in playing time, supported player counts, and the experiences they offer.

02 / DESIGN

More Mechanics, New Possibilities

Board-game design has shifted towards combining more gameplay mechanics. While around 80% of games before 1980 used only 1–2 mechanics, this fell to approximately 45% in 2010–2019. Games with more mechanics also tend to receive higher player ratings, although complexity alone does not guarantee quality.

03 / GLOBAL REACH

From Local Tables to Global Communities

Board-game cafés and crowdfunding campaigns reveal an international hobby. Yet this growth is uneven: the United States accounts for 8,499 Kickstarter campaigns and approximately US$254.3 million in funding in the analysed data, highlighting the concentration of crowdfunding activity.

THE FINAL TAKEAWAY

Board games have evolved beyond the rules, bringing new experiences and connecting players worldwide.

From simpler classics to modern strategic games, the hobby continues to grow, innovate, and bring people together.
